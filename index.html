Alex Rivera

Department of Public Policy, Whitfield University — Expected completion, May 2027

My research examines how street-level administrative choices shape who actually receives public benefits — combining large-scale administrative data with fieldwork inside state welfare offices. I study the gap between policy as written and policy as delivered, and what that gap costs the people it's meant to serve.

Job Market Paper

Governing at the Margins: Administrative Discretion and Equity in Safety-Net Delivery

Working paper — under review

Using ten years of caseworker-level administrative records from three state SNAP agencies, I show that discretionary processing decisions — not eligibility rules — account for a substantial share of racial disparities in benefit approval speed. I identify the organizational conditions under which discretion narrows rather than widens these gaps.

Street-level bureaucracy Administrative burden Causal inference
Working Paper

Waiting as Policy: Queue Design and Take-Up in Means-Tested Programs

Prepared for submission, Journal of Public Administration Research and Theory

A field experiment with a mid-size county benefits office testing how appointment scheduling design affects application completion among first-time applicants, with attention to compounding effects for non-English speakers.

Field experiment Program take-up Language access

Algorithmic Triage in Public Agencies: A Comparative Case Study

Fieldwork ongoing — three state agencies

Comparative case study of algorithmic risk-scoring tools adopted by three state child welfare agencies, tracing how frontline staff interpret, override, or defer to automated recommendations in practice.
